PBF = (Body Fat Mass ÷ Weight) × 100. This is the most direct measure of body fat percentage. Unlike BMI, it directly measures the proportion of the body that is fat tissue. InBody measures this via bioelectrical impedance — far more accurate than BMI alone.

05 ECW/TBW Ratio Borderline

What This Ratio Measures

ECW/TBW is the ratio of Extracellular Water (water outside cells) to Total Body Water (all fluids). It's a sensitive marker of fluid balance, inflammation, and cellular health.

The Biology

Healthy cells hold more water inside (ICW). When cells are inflamed, stressed, or when edema (swelling) occurs, water shifts outside the cells — raising the ECW/TBW ratio. A rising ratio can indicate:

  • Chronic inflammation
  • Overtraining or injury
  • Poor nutrition or high sodium intake
  • Certain medical conditions (heart, kidney, liver)
  • Pre-menstrual fluid retention

09 Basal Metabolic Rate Normal

What Is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest — just to keep organs functioning, maintain body temperature, and keep you alive. It does NOT include any activity.

1,231
kcal / day at rest

How InBody Calculates BMR

InBody uses lean body mass (not total weight) to calculate BMR — which is more accurate than height/weight formulas. Muscle is metabolically active: every pound of muscle you gain adds roughly 6–10 kcal/day to your BMR. Building 9 lbs of muscle would increase her BMR by ~60–90 kcal/day.

Using BMR for Nutrition Planning

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

BMR × Activity Factor = estimated total daily calories burned:

Practical Coaching Application

For fat loss + muscle gain (recomposition), target TDEE minus 200–300 kcal with high protein (0.7–1g per lb of goal body weight). A 300 kcal deficit × 7 days = 2,100 kcal deficit per week ≈ 0.6 lbs fat loss per week.

10 Visceral Fat Level High — Priority

What Is Visceral Fat?

Visceral fat is fat stored deep inside the abdomen, surrounding internal organs like the liver, pancreas, and intestines. Unlike subcutaneous fat (under the skin), it's not visible or pinchable — but it has a much greater impact on metabolic health than surface-level fat.

Why Visceral Fat Is Dangerous

High visceral fat is directly linked to:

  • Type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance
  • Cardiovascular disease and high blood pressure
  • Chronic systemic inflammation
  • Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
  • Certain cancers

How to Reduce Visceral Fat — Coaching Priorities

Cardio Training

Moderate-intensity cardio (30–45 min, 3–5x/week) is the most effective training stimulus for reducing visceral fat. Zone 2 (conversational pace) is ideal for sustained fat oxidation.

Nutrition

Reduce processed carbohydrates and added sugars. These cause insulin spikes that directly promote visceral fat storage. Prioritize fiber, protein, and healthy fats.

Sleep & Stress

Poor sleep and chronic stress elevate cortisol, which directly drives visceral fat accumulation. 7–9 hours of sleep is non-negotiable for fat loss clients.

11 Impedance Values Raw Data

How InBody Measures You

The InBody570 uses Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA) — it sends a safe, low-level electrical current through the body and measures the resistance (impedance) it encounters. Different tissues resist electricity differently:

Why Two Frequencies?

  • 5 kHz (low freq): Current travels outside cells — measures extracellular fluid and fat (poor conductors)
  • 500 kHz (high freq): Current penetrates cell membranes — measures total body water including intracellular fluid

By comparing the two frequencies, InBody can calculate how much water is inside vs. outside cells — giving you the ICW/ECW breakdown.

Why the Trunk Impedance Is So Low

The trunk shows values around 21–30 Ω — far lower than the arms (400–500 Ω) or legs (300–600 Ω). This is because the trunk is a large, fluid-filled cavity with a short path length. High fluid = low resistance.

What Affects Impedance Readings

Testing conditions that can skew results:

  • Eating or drinking within 2 hours before test
  • Exercise within 12 hours before test
  • Menstrual cycle phase (fluid retention)
  • Dehydration or alcohol consumption
  • Lotion, socks, or bare metal jewelry on test points

Always test clients under the same conditions for comparable results.

12 Results Interpretation Guide Reference

Body Fat Analysis Interpretation

BMI uses height and weight to flag potential weight-related health risk — but it can't distinguish muscle from fat. A muscular athlete can have a high BMI while having low body fat. Always use PBF alongside BMI for a complete picture. InBody's PBF directly measures body fat percentage via impedance, making it significantly more useful than BMI alone for fitness clients.

Segmental Lean Analysis Interpretation

The top bar in each segment shows how much lean mass the client actually has. The bottom bar shows the ideal amount. When the top bar is shorter than the bottom bar, the muscle is underdeveloped relative to the client's size. When muscles are adequately developed (top bar meets or exceeds bottom bar), the segment is said to be "in balance."

Body Water Analysis Interpretation

ECW/TBW is the ratio of Extracellular Water to Total Body Water. It's an important indicator of whether the body's water distribution is balanced. A healthy ratio is approximately 0.360–0.380. Values above 0.380 may indicate inflammation, injury, or edema. Values persistently above 0.400 should prompt a referral to a physician to rule out systemic causes.

Visceral Fat Level Interpretation

Visceral Fat Level (VFL) is an estimated indicator based on the amount of fat surrounding internal organs in the abdomen. A VFL under 10 is associated with healthy metabolic function. A VFL of 10 or above suggests elevated visceral fat — the type most associated with metabolic syndrome. Diet, cardiovascular exercise, sleep, and stress management are all effective ways to bring this number down.
